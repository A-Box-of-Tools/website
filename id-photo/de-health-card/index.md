# Photo requirements: Health insurance card, Germany

The published rule for this document’s photograph — from Techniker Krankenkasse, as it was read on 2026-09-21 — and a way to make one that meets it without sending your face to anybody.

Last updated 2026-09-21

## The figures

| What is measured | What this rule publishes |
| --- | --- |
| Print size | ⁦35 x 45⁩ mm at 300 dpi |
| Head, chin to crown | 70.0% to 80.0% (⁦31.5–36⁩ mm) (guidance) |
| Eye line, from the bottom | 50.0% to 60.0% (⁦22.5–27⁩ mm) (guidance) |
| Background | Plain and light — no colour is published |
| Upload rule | none published — this one is a print |

## What else this rule says

- The photograph on a German health card does not have to be biometric, and every statutory insurer publishes its own wording; this is the Techniker Krankenkasse’s. ⁦35 x 45⁩ mm, colour or black and white, a background “as neutral as possible”, no hat or cap, and a digital picture of at least ⁦300 x 400⁩ pixels as a JPG, PNG or BMP. Check your own insurer’s page before uploading: some take the picture in their app instead.
- No head-height band is published for this one, so the ICAO figures are shown in its place and are never marked as a failure. Frame it the way the rule describes in words and the numbers follow.

## What the tool does with them

- Locks the crop to this document’s shape, and draws the head and eye bands inside it, so you can see the rule on your own photograph instead of reading about it.
- Finds the crown, the chin and both pupils by measuring the picture — no face model is downloaded — and says in millimetres whether the head is too large or too small for this rule.
- Reads the background colour and how evenly it is lit, and says which of the two is the problem when there is one.
- Writes the print at the exact millimetres with the resolution in the file, a sheet of them with cut marks, and the upload file at the pixel and file size this rule states.
