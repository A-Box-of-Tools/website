    2 Crop, if you want to

    The box starts on the whole picture, so leaving this step alone crops nothing. Every image keeps its own box — pick one from the list above to draw on it. Cropping happens before resizing, which is the only order that makes sense: a picture cut down after it has been shrunk has already thrown away the pixels it needed.

    Add an image and the box appears here.

    Drag inside the box to move it, or any corner to resize. With the box focused, the arrow keys nudge it a pixel at a time, Alt + arrows resize it, and holding Shift makes each step ten.

    3 Say what size it should be

    Fill in one and leave the other blank to keep the picture's own shape.

    Whichever side is longer becomes this, and the other follows. Portrait and landscape shots in the same batch all come out the same size on their long edge, which is what a gallery usually wants.

    4 Save it

    JPEG and WebP only; PNG has no quality dial. 85 is where most people stop being able to tell. Below about 60 the flat areas start to band.

    What shows through where the picture does not: behind a padded frame, and behind transparency when the output format has no alpha channel.
