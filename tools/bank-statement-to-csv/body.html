Taking the password off, on your own machine

Banks often lock a statement with your date of birth or the last digits of an account number. Any of these gives you an unlocked copy; bring it back here and it will still have its text, which is what this tool needs.

  • The PDF Unlocker on this site — type the password there and it comes off in this browser, with nothing uploaded. It is the only one of these that rewrites nothing on the page, so the words stay exactly where your bank put them.
  • Windows — open the PDF in Chrome or Edge, type the password, then Print and choose Save as PDF as the printer.
  • macOS — open it in Preview, type the password, then File → Export as PDF.

Do not use any other website that offers to unlock it. That means uploading the statement, which is the one thing this page exists to avoid. The page linked above is not one of those: it has no server to upload anything to, and it keeps working with the network unplugged.

This one is a photograph, and what to do instead

There is no text in the file — its pages are pictures, from a scanner or a phone. Reading letters out of a picture is a different job from reading a table out of text, and this tool does not pretend to do it. In rough order of how well they work:

  • Ask your bank for the data instead of the paper. Nearly every online bank will hand you the same statement as CSV, OFX or QIF. That skips this page altogether and is more accurate than any conversion of any picture could be.
  • Ask whoever sent it for the original PDF rather than a scan of the printout.
  • Or read the picture on your own machine. Windows' Snipping Tool, the Live Text built into macOS, and Acrobat's Recognise Text all do it without uploading anything. Then check every amount by eye: a misread digit does not look like an error, it looks like a number.

2 Check what it read

A PDF looks as though it holds tables and does not: it holds text placed at positions on a page, and the columns are strips of paper nobody printed in. So every table below was worked out rather than read. Where one of them carries a running balance, the line above the tables is the tool checking its own work — every row proved against the row before it.
