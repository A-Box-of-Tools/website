Taking the password off, on your own machine

Banks often lock a statement with your date of birth or the last digits of an account number. Opening it once with the password and saving a fresh copy is enough — the copy has no password and still has its text, which is what this tool needs.

  • Windows — open the PDF in Chrome or Edge, type the password, then Print and choose Save as PDF as the printer.
  • macOS — open it in Preview, type the password, then File → Export as PDF.

Do not use a website that offers to unlock it. That means uploading the statement, which is the one thing this page exists to avoid.

This one is a photograph, and what to do instead

There is no text in the file — its pages are pictures, from a scanner or a phone. Reading letters out of a picture is a different job from reading a table out of text, and this tool does not pretend to do it. In rough order of how well they work:

  • Ask your bank for the data instead of the paper. Nearly every online bank will hand you the same statement as CSV, OFX or QIF. That skips this page altogether and is more accurate than any conversion of any picture could be.
  • Ask whoever sent it for the original PDF rather than a scan of the printout.
  • Or read the picture on your own machine. Windows' Snipping Tool, the Live Text built into macOS, and Acrobat's Recognise Text all do it without uploading anything. Then check every amount by eye: a misread digit does not look like an error, it looks like a number.
