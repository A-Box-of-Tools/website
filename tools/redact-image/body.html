1 Choose the image

A screenshot of a bank statement, a photo of a passport, a bill with an address on it — the pictures people need to redact are exactly the pictures they should not be uploading to a stranger's server to have it done. This one is read straight off your disk by the browser.

2 Cover what should not be seen

Choose an image and it appears here to draw on.

Drag across the picture to draw a box. Drag a box to move it, drag its handles to resize it, or press Tab to reach one and use the arrow keys — Alt and the arrows resize, Delete removes. What you see under each box is the real thing: the preview is drawn by the same code that writes the file, at screen size.

What a new box does

    3 Save the redacted copy

    The picture is decoded to pixels, the boxes are written over those pixels, and the result is encoded as a new file. There is no layer, no annotation and no record of where the boxes were, because the covered pixels stop existing before the encoder is handed anything.

    It makes no difference to the redaction: the pixels are already gone before the encoder sees them. It only decides what the rest of the picture costs and how it is compressed.
