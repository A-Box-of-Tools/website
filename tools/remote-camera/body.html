    How cameras are listed

    Choose a camera to fill its code, then select Connect to camera when you are ready. Connecting shares network addresses. Its owner may require approval or accept viewers automatically.

    The network list matches the internet address seen by our introduction service. A shared VPN or carrier network can include strangers, and nearby cameras can be missed. Private groups instead match a shared code and can list cameras on different networks. Both Direct and Cloud relay sessions appear; icons show their route and access requirements. Choose a camera you recognise.

    Both devices need this page open and internet for pairing. Direct uses a local network that lets the devices reach each other. Cloud relay carries encrypted video and optional audio through the relay service when both people choose it. Nothing is recorded by this tool.

    Share my camera

    Allow camera access on this device. The other device opens your link or scans the QR code. Choose whether you want to approve each viewer.

    About the viewer link name

    Choose your own name using letters, numbers and hyphens, or keep the random suggestion. Stop before changing the name. Your browser chooses the camera.

    The link name is visible in the network or private group selected above. Private group sessions are not also listed in the network list. Turn this off to invite viewers by link only.

    About viewer approval

    Approve viewer requests on this device. Changes to this setting apply to new requests.

    About enabling the microphone

    Audio is off by default. Enable it before starting to request microphone access. Stop sharing before changing this choice. If the browser interrupts the microphone, choose Resume microphone when you are ready.

    About sharing video bandwidth

    Choose a video limit that leaves room for other Wi-Fi traffic. Up to eight viewers share it; each connection can lower its quality when congested. The browser cannot measure your total Wi-Fi capacity. Audio and network overhead are additional. A 1 Mbps limit allows four new viewers; higher limits allow eight. Lowering the limit keeps current viewers connected.

    About your viewer name

    Optional. This note goes to the camera browser over the encrypted connection.

    About connecting to a camera

    Connecting shares each browser’s network address with the other and contacts our introduction service. The camera may ask its owner to approve you or start video automatically, depending on its setting. Continue only if you know who controls it.

    Enter a camera code to request a view.

    About camera audio and video controls

    Audio starts automatically when the host enables its microphone and your browser allows playback. Otherwise, choose Unmute audio. Muting playback does not stop sound detection. Full screen keeps these controls with the video.

    About motion and sound notifications

    Enable an alert to request notification permission. Detection runs on this device while this page stays open; browsers may pause it in the background. Lighting changes can trigger motion alerts. Sound alerts need the host to share audio and work while playback is muted. Alerts can be missed; they are not a safety monitor.

    A viewer wants to join

    Approve only a person or device you recognise. Review later or Escape keeps this request in Viewer requests without approving it.

    Stop sharing your camera?

    Viewers are connected or connecting to this camera. Stopping will disconnect them and end the viewer link.
    Connection details and troubleshooting

    No camera connection yet.

    Direct offers only the browsers’ own host addresses, without public STUN servers or TURN relays. A VPN or operating-system route can affect the path. Guest Wi-Fi isolation, a firewall or local-network permissions can prevent Direct. You can start a new Cloud relay session to send encrypted traffic through the relay service; the viewer must explicitly connect using that choice. A managed device can block either mode.

    If pairing fails, stop on both devices, check that both are on the same reachable network, allow local-network access if asked, and try a new link. The camera device needs trusted HTTPS; ordinary HTTP on a LAN IP cannot request a camera.

    A connection report includes browser and tool versions, stages, counts and error names, without addresses, pairing codes, viewer notes, video or audio. It stays in this tab. Copy or download it to share it yourself; nothing is uploaded automatically.
