1 Write it, or attach it

Files ride the same direct channel as the text, up to 200 MB apiece. Readers see the list and fetch each file only if they ask for it.

2 Name the link

The name is the address and the only secret: short enough to say across a room, and worth making unguessable for anything private.

The draft is kept only in this browser, so it is still here next time — unless you mark it one-off, in which case nothing is kept anywhere at all.

3 Share it

Nothing is stored anywhere

The text and the files travel straight from this tab to each reader’s, encrypted. The one server involved introduces the two browsers and never sees what they exchange. Closing this tab ends the share and clears readers’ open pages too — though what someone already copied or downloaded is theirs.

Someone is sharing with you

Looking for the sharer…

Reading it connects your browser straight to the sharer’s — that is the point: nothing here touches a server. Like any direct connection, it lets each side see the other’s network (IP) address. Nothing is fetched, and nothing about you is shared, until you choose to connect.

Tell the sharer something they’ll recognise — a name, a hint — and they can let you in. Your message goes straight to their browser.
