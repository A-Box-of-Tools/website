2 Say what the shape is

Tracing needs one bit per pixel — in, or out. There are two ways to decide that, and they suit different pictures. A drawing on white paper divides by light and dark. A photograph of one object does not: a dark red figure on dark grey stone is dark on dark, and no brightness divides them. That one wants the subject, which works out what the background is from the edges of the picture and keeps whatever is not it.

3 Check the outline, and fix what it got wrong

The red line is the outline as it stands, drawn over the pixels it came from — which is the only place the question can be settled, because an outline is right or wrong relative to those pixels and nothing else. Drag either picture to move both, roll the wheel to zoom, and click anything that should not be there. A click takes the whole patch of that colour, so you are pointing at a shape rather than a pixel; click it again to put it back.

How smooth the line should be

Two numbers decide how the staircase of pixels becomes curves. Neither needs touching for most pictures, and both are worth knowing about when one does.

How far the line may stray from the pixels. Below about one it does nothing at all, because a staircase step stands a whole pixel off the line it belongs to; above about two it starts eating real curves.

How far the outline must turn before that turn is kept as a corner rather than rounded into a curve. Low enough and every wobble is a corner, and a circle comes back as a polygon.
