1 Say who you are comparing

A name, a height, and what kind of figure to draw. Every row starts at a height you can type straight over. Heights can be written however you write them — 173, 1.73 m, 5'8", 68 in — and the line underneath each box shows what was understood, or says so in red if the box is empty or the height cannot be read. Nothing is ever guessed silently.

Top to bottom here is left to right on the chart. Drag a row by the handle at its left edge to move it, or use the arrows, which do the same thing from a keyboard. Either way the chart goes back to your own order if it was sorting by height.

Your own SVG puts any drawing on the ruler — a logo, a floor plan, a piece of machinery. It is read in this page and goes nowhere. Only the shapes in it are kept: it arrives as one solid colour you pick per row, and anything that could reach the network — a linked image, a stylesheet, a font, a script — is left behind rather than carried into a chart you might send to somebody. A drawing made of thin lines rather than filled shapes will come out as a solid blob, which is the one case where this is the wrong tool.

A few of these really are fixed: a 20 ft shipping container is 259 cm tall by definition, a basketball rim is 305, and an American interior door is 203 by 81. The rest are typical rather than standard — doors, windows and worktops vary by country and by house. Every one arrives as an ordinary row, so measure yours and type the real numbers over it.

2 Take it

The PNG is the one to paste into a document or a chat. The SVG is the one to keep: it is the chart as instructions rather than as pixels, so it prints at any size and can be recoloured later by anything that opens vectors.
