Your files

Files selected for processing are read, edited and written inside the browser on your device. abox.tools has no file-processing upload endpoint or database of those files. Downloads are assembled locally. Sharing, remote camera viewing and handing a receipt report to another app are deliberate transfers, described below.

Local processing does not give us:

  • your files or their previews
  • their filenames, sizes, dimensions or formats
  • their contents, including EXIF, GPS and recognised document text

Each page carries a Content-Security-Policy that limits permitted resources and destinations. Some tool policies add the rendezvous or the optional exchange-rate API. A policy does not inspect the data sent to an allowed host, and connect-src does not describe WebRTC peer traffic. Read it alongside the source and observe the feature you are using, rather than treating the host list as proof of every payload.

Files you produce with a tool are handed to your browser's own download mechanism and saved wherever you tell it to. We are not involved in that step either.

Features that deliberately use a connection

Images to Video can fetch an image from a web address you supply. That server receives your IP address and the requested URL, including query parameters. The image is copied into local memory and re-encoded for processing. This request does not send files selected from your device.

Receipt & Invoice Extractor reads photos and recognises text locally. Its optional historical-rate button requests a currency pair and your chosen date from Frankfurter. The date may be the date printed on a receipt. The provider sees your IP address and request; the request omits credentials and referrers and contains no photo, filename, merchant, amount or email address. Manual rates require no request.

Receipt email and sharing buttons prepare reports and JPEG copies locally, then download an email file or hand them to your chosen device app. This page does not send an email. The app, provider and recipient you choose handle the next step under their policies. Share Text & Files, Share a Browser Tab and Remote Camera deliberately send content to another browser; their connection metadata is described below.

What is collected, and by whom

This site is free and is paid for by advertising. That means two Google products run on these pages, and a donate button runs on most of them. Here is the whole list.

Google AdSense — the ads

Google serves the ads and decides which ones you see. To do that it may set and read cookies or similar identifiers in your browser, and it receives your IP address, an approximate location derived from it, your user agent, and which page you were on. Depending on your settings and where you are, the ads may be personalised using a profile Google holds about you, built largely from your activity on other sites.

We do not receive any of that, we cannot see it, and we never send Google anything about your files. Google's own account of how it uses data from sites that run its ads is at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

Google Analytics — visits and successful tool use

We use Google Analytics 4 to count page visits and successful tool use, so we know which tools are worth working on. It records the page you viewed, roughly when, a randomly generated identifier stored in your browser, an approximate location, your device and browser type, and standard campaign attribution tags.

The page address sent to Analytics uses its canonical URL with only standard campaign tags. Other query parameters and address fragments are omitted. The page title is fixed, and the referring site is reduced to its origin, without its path or query.

We count the first successful tool use per page load, such as requesting a ready download, completing a copy, or displaying a usable analysis or view in an inspector. The only tool-specific detail added to this event is the tool's fixed identifier. It does not confirm that your operating system saved a download.

Optional feedback records the fixed tool identifier, a fixed up or down answer and a fixed reason after you choose an answer. There is no free-text field. The tools attach no files, filenames, contents, entered text, counts, sizes, dimensions, formats, settings, errors or other input or output details to either record. We can view aggregate Analytics reports. Blocking Analytics leaves local processing available, including offline use once the tool's cache is ready.

Buy Me a Coffee — the donate button

The hub page and the tool pages carry a donate button, which loads from Buy Me a Coffee's servers. Loading it means their CDN sees your IP address and that you were on this site, and the button's lettering is fetched from Google Fonts, which likewise sees your IP address. Nothing else is sent, and nothing further happens unless you actually click it, at which point you are on their site under their policies. This page and the Terms page do not draw the button.

Hosting

The site is served by GitHub Pages, behind Cloudflare. Like any web host, they process the requests your browser makes — which includes your IP address, the page requested, and your user agent — in order to deliver the page and to keep the service up and secure. We do not have access to per-visitor logs from either of them.

Share a Browser Tab

Share a Browser Tab uses a desktop browser’s picker to capture one tab and send live video to one approved viewer. The viewer requests access, the host approves and the viewer acknowledges before video is sent. There is no audio, camera or microphone capture. Information visible in the chosen tab is shared; the viewer can record what they receive.

Online nearby discovery is off by default. The host may enable Show in nearby shared tabs before starting; a viewer explicitly chooses Find nearby tabs to open the list. The discovery connection lists only the random invitation code, never the captured tab’s title, address, picture or viewer note. Selecting a result fills the invitation and still requires Request to watch and approval. The service groups this tool’s pages by public IPv4 address or IPv6 /64 subnet and website origin, not physical location. Shared gateways or VPNs can include strangers or hide nearby devices. Listings live on open connections without stored history, and stopping withdraws the host’s listing.

Video uses a direct encrypted connection on a reachable local network, without public STUN or TURN. Discovery and pairing need internet access on both devices. The rendezvous sees room codes, IP addresses, times, WebRTC descriptions and candidates, but not video, viewer notes or approval decisions. Cloudflare retains connection metadata logs for seven days.

The page’s normal scripts can read its address and state, including an invitation code; a link alone never grants video access. The viewer cannot control the host or select webpage text. The tool has no recording feature, but visible signed-in content is part of the picture and the viewer can independently capture or record it. The tool page explains its network limits; local address settings do not prove a physical network boundary.

Remote Camera and browser introductions

Remote Camera opens a discovery WebSocket when its page loads; Start camera or Connect adds a room connection to our rendezvous. Choosing a listed name fills its code and waits for Connect, which requests a connection and exchanges network addresses. Opening a viewer link still waits for Connect. Viewer approval is on by default and controls transmitted video and audio, not address exchange. The rendezvous sees codes, addresses, timings and setup messages, never video, audio or viewer notes. Video and enabled audio use an encrypted direct browser connection with no configured STUN server or TURN relay and no recording feature. Audio is off by default; the host requests microphone access only after choosing to share audio. Internet is still required for introductions. Routing can be affected by VPNs and operating-system policy; local mode does not prove a physical network boundary. The tool page explains these limits and how page scripts can read the viewer-link address.

Optional viewer alerts analyze small video frames and received audio on the viewer device without recordings or uploads. Enabling alerts requests browser notification permission. Generic notices contain no camera image, audio, link name or viewer note; the browser and operating system display them. Disconnect stops analysis and closes this session’s notifications. The viewer page must stay open; browsers may pause analysis in the background or during sleep.

Local Walkie-Talkie

Local Walkie-Talkie hosts directly in the website without Python or a download. Opening the page connects securely to our rendezvous for nearby discovery. Starting or joining adds a channel connection for authentication, setup and availability checks. Show this channel nearby publishes its name and listener invitation to browsers grouped by public IPv4 address or IPv6 /64 subnet and page origin. Shared gateways can include strangers and VPNs can hide nearby devices; this is not a physical Wi-Fi scan. Hosts can turn the option off before starting to keep invitations link-only. Hiding or ending the host withdraws the listing. Live voice travels directly from the host browser to up to ten receive-only browsers using encrypted WebRTC; the rendezvous receives no audio and writes no room history. Temporary room state lives on open connections. Cloudflare retains seven-day invocation metadata logs including the room identifier, address and timing. Host and listener keys differ and travel in secure message payloads, not request URLs. Listener invitations carry a fragment key and the tool retains temporary per-tab identities in session storage for recovery. The site's regular page scripts can read its address and browser storage; removing a fragment after saving it does not isolate them. Anyone with a listener invitation and direct reachability can listen. No STUN server, TURN relay or recording feature is used. Internet pairing is required; VPN and operating-system routes can affect direct reachability without proving a physical LAN boundary. A recipient can independently record audio.

Voice Oncall

Voice Oncall receives separate direct encrypted audio streams from up to ten participants in one host browser. Its nearby discovery connection lists advertised pagers sharing a public IPv4 address or IPv6 subnet and page origin; this is a hint, not a LAN scan or proof of physical Wi-Fi membership. The service sees pager names, password-derived admission credentials, moderation status and connection details, never voice. Callers need no display name; the host sees a short identifier instead. Older clients may still send a display name. An optional password controls admission without a host approval prompt. It is derived in the browser and its access credential is sent inside secure signaling rather than in a URL. The host never grants microphone access; participants deliberately enable transmission with Talk. Host mute silences each participant's playback locally, and removal blocks its retained session identity rather than a permanent person-level identity. Temporary credentials stay in per-tab session storage when available; the site's normal scripts can read the page address and that storage. Socket attachments hold temporary pager state without stored pager history, while Cloudflare keeps seven-day invocation metadata logs. No STUN server, TURN relay or recording feature is configured. Internet is required for discovery and pairing, and direct reachability still depends on browser and network routes.

Share Text & Files and the rendezvous

Share Text & Files opens a discovery connection on its start page and another connection when sharing. Names are grouped by public IPv4 address or IPv6 subnet and page origin; this is a discovery hint, not proof of the same physical network. The rendezvous sees names, IP addresses, connection times and negotiation messages, never shared text or files. Advertised names live on open connections without durable storage. Ordinary mode uses public STUN servers from Google and Cloudflare; a reader may explicitly retry through a Cloudflare TURN relay, which forwards encrypted traffic without its decryption keys. Local network mode omits STUN and TURN, but still needs the public rendezvous. Cloudflare keeps seven-day connection metadata logs for the share, camera and voice rendezvous; these are per-visitor service logs we can read. Content copied or downloaded by a recipient remains theirs after the source tab closes.

Cookies

We set no login or session cookies. Google scripts may set cookies and identifiers as described above. The site also uses local browser storage for preferences and temporary work.

The language you choose. If you pick a language from the switcher, that choice is written to your browser's local storage, under the name abox-lang, so that the next page you open is in the language you asked for. It is not a cookie: it is never sent to us or to anybody else, it stays on the device you are reading this on, and clearing your browser's site data removes it. If you never pick a language, nothing is written at all — a page shown in your browser's own language was matched on the spot and forgotten again.

Other local storage. Feedback stores an answer or dismissal timestamp to avoid asking again. Tools may keep settings or an optional sharing draft locally. A language switch or an explicit carry-on action can temporarily store files and settings in IndexedDB so another local tool page can pick them up. These records are not cookies and are not sent to us; clearing site data removes them. The relevant tool explains persistent drafts.

Google's cookies and identifiers are used to measure visits and tool use and to select and cap ads. They are distinct from the local site records described above.

How to switch it off

  • Ad personalisation can be turned off, for all sites at once, at My Ad Center.
  • Google Analytics can be blocked everywhere with Google's opt-out browser add-on.
  • Your browser's own settings can block or clear third-party cookies, and any content blocker will stop these scripts loading in the first place.

Blocking advertising, Analytics and the donate button leaves local processing available. Once a tool's offline cache is ready, its local processing works disconnected. Web-image imports, online rates, browser introductions and delivery through another app need their respective connections. No processing feature is withheld behind an ad.

Your rights over the data

We do not hold files you process. Analytics reports and the rendezvous connection metadata described above are different data. Questions or requests about the site's handling of these can go to hi@abox.tools; identify the page and approximate date, without attaching a private file.

For data in your Google account, use your account controls or Google's privacy contacts. Optional exchange-rate, donation and email providers handle their own services and requests.
