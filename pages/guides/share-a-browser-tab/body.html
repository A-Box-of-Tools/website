Choose the tab, then invite the viewer

Open the website in one tab and Share a Browser Tab in another on your computer. Choose Choose a tab to share and select the website tab in your browser's own picker. Choose a browser tab, rather than a window or your whole screen: this tool refuses those broader sources. Check the local preview before sending the link.

Choose Copy viewer link and send it to the other person. They open it, read the connection notice, optionally add a short introduction and choose Request to watch. Return to the sharing page to approve the request you expect. The connection first carries only encrypted control messages; video begins after your approval and the viewer's acknowledgement. One viewer can watch at a time, with up to four requests waiting.

Work in the selected website tab while keeping the sharing page open. A current desktop Chrome or Edge browser is the intended host. The viewer needs a browser that can play WebRTC video, without an extension or an account on the website you show. Both devices need internet for the introduction.

What the viewer can see and do

The viewer sees a live video picture. Your scrolling, page navigation, menus and visible account content in that tab become part of the picture. Simply activating another browser tab does not select a new capture source, but anything you reveal inside the selected tab is shared. Check the browser's capture indicator and stop before opening private information there.

The viewer's clicks and typing cannot operate your browser. They do not receive your login cookies or a working copy of the website. Text in a video is not selectable, so this version cannot provide webpage copying. Use Share Text & Files alongside it when the other person needs a link, note or file they can copy. Some protected content may be black or unavailable in a capture.

No audio is requested or sent, including tab sound. The tool does not request camera or microphone access and makes no recording. A viewer can still record their own view or take screenshots. Ending the link stops future video; it cannot erase anything already kept.

The link permits a request; your approval permits video

Each session has a fresh random link and is not listed in a discovery directory. Anyone who obtains the link can request a connection, including the network-address exchange needed to establish it. They still need your approval for video. The name or note they type is not verified identity, so recognise the person through the conversation you already have and approve only the request you expect.

A URL fragment does not hide a link code from scripts running in the same page, and a browser may keep its history. The tool page has the site's usual advertising, measurement and donation scripts. The tool does not deliberately send them video or notes, but same-page scripts can read the page's address and state. Mandatory approval remains the admission step.

How the video reaches the other browser

A small introduction service matches the browsers through the random code and forwards WebRTC setup messages. It sees the code, addresses, connection times, descriptions and connection candidates. Setup is not hidden from that service, but the video, viewer note and approval decisions do not pass through it. Cloudflare keeps connection request metadata for seven days. The service writes no room content to storage.

The browsers try a direct encrypted WebRTC connection first, using public STUN services to find reachable addresses. Each browser learns network addresses from the other before video approval. When a direct path fails, the viewer can explicitly choose a fresh attempt through Cloudflare's TURN relay. It forwards encrypted traffic without the media keys and sees addresses, timing and traffic volume. The tool never selects that relay automatically.

A firewall, VPN, browser policy or restrictive network can block either path. Try the actual connection before a demonstration or support call, and keep an agreed fallback. A missing relay configuration is a visible failure, rather than a promise that every network can connect.

Keep both host tabs open, then stop deliberately

Hiding the sharing page keeps healthy capture where the browser permits, which lets you use the selected website tab normally. Keep both tabs open and the device awake. Sleep, a closed laptop or browser suspension can interrupt or end the stream; this page cannot override those policies.

Choose Stop sharing on the tool page or use your browser's capture control when finished. Closing the sharing page or ending the captured tab also ends the source. If capture ends, returning to the page never silently captures again. Start again, choose a tab again and send the new invitation. A viewer who disconnects leaves the host's selected source available, but a new viewer still needs fresh approval.
