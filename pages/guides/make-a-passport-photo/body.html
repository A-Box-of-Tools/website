Read the rule for your application before editing

Start with the issuing authority's instructions for the document and application route. A printed passport photo, an online renewal, a visa and a staff badge can ask for different things, even in the same country. Dimensions are only part of the rule: how the photo was taken and whether it may be edited can decide whether a home-made copy is usable at all.

  • UK online passport applications. GOV.UK says to leave a device photo uncropped; the application does that for you, and the photo must be unaltered by software. Upload the original that meets its requirements instead of an export from this tool.
  • Canadian passports. Canada requires a commercial photographer for paper applications and online renewals. Paper photos must be professionally printed; home prints and altered photos are not accepted. A crop tool cannot replace that process.
  • US passports. The paper-application photo guide calls for an original, unedited photo and prohibits changes with software, apps or filters. Online renewal has its own digital-photo instructions. Follow the route you are using.

These are examples of why a country preset is a measurement aid rather than permission to edit a submission. Check the current official page linked from the selected specification, including any rules for children or medical needs, before using the export.

Take a usable source photograph

Where the issuer permits taking the photograph yourself, have somebody else hold the camera at eye level rather than taking an arm's-length selfie. Leave room around the head and shoulders so that the permitted framing can be chosen later. Start with the original camera file, not a screenshot of it or a copy downloaded from a chat.

Use a plain background in the colour the issuer asks for and even light from in front. Move away from the wall until your head casts no visible shadow on it. Check focus on the eyes and avoid clipped highlights, glare and hair obscuring the face. If the source is wrong, retake it: the tool does not replace a background, straighten a tilted head or remove a shadow.

The frame and the head are separate measurements

Open the ID Photo Maker, choose the country and the exact document, and read the specification panel. It names the print size, head-height band, eye position, background and upload limits held by that preset, with a source and the date it was checked. Figures shown as guidance are not mandatory limits. Compare them with the current application instructions before relying on them.

The frame can be the right size while the face inside it is too large or too small. Head height is measured from the chin to the crown defined by that document's instructions. Do not assume that crown always includes all the hair: some rules measure the skull instead. The eye line, where specified, is a separate position within the frame rather than a second way of measuring head height.

Check the four marks before fitting the box

The tool suggests a crown, a chin and two pupil positions from the photograph. The line underneath says which it measured and which it estimated. Inspect all four; a neat overlay is not evidence that the points landed on the right features.

Drag a misplaced dot, or choose I'll place them and position all four yourself. The dots can also be reached with Tab and moved with the arrow keys; Shift moves ten source pixels at a time. Then press Fit the box and inspect head height, eye line, centring and tilt. If the crop needs enlarging beyond the source's available pixels, use a better source rather than expecting the extra pixels to restore detail.

The background check measures colour and evenness separately. It cannot decide whether the expression, clothing, glasses or an exemption meets an authority's rules. Use that authority's examples for the visual check, and retain the source file alongside any prepared copy.

A visa, a badge or a signature has its own destination

A visa application. Choose the visa's specification rather than a passport preset from the same country. Check that the issuer permits the preparation you intend, then compare the final dimensions and byte size with the actual upload form. A photo that worked for another document is not automatically the right source.

A staff, student or event badge. When its issuer permits a prepared portrait, choose Anywhere else and enter its supplied print, head-height and upload requirements. Read back the entered figures before exporting. If the job is simply a rectangular portrait with a pixel size and no face measurements, the Image Resizer gives you that crop without inventing a passport rule for a badge.

A separate signature upload. Use the signature entry matching your examination notice, and compare its displayed guidance with that notice. Crop to the ink on plain paper, keeping ruled lines and the page edge outside the box. Inspect fine strokes in the downloaded JPEG. A signature is not a face and should not be fitted with the portrait's crown and pupil marks.

Choose the file meant for the next step

A permitted export can produce three different files. The print carries the chosen physical dimensions and DPI. The sheet puts copies on the selected paper with cut marks in the gaps. The upload uses the preset's pixel dimensions and file-size band. Send the upload file to a form, not the sheet of repeated portraits.

There is no universal portal limit. Some forms set both a minimum and a maximum byte size; some pixel dimensions in the presets are advisory. Read the current notice or form rather than copying a figure from another examination or a paper-photo rule. If the JPEG falls below a requested minimum even at the highest quality, the tool can add a JPEG comment segment without changing its image pixels. That meets a byte count, not an acceptance guarantee; another application may re-encode the file and remove the padding.

Check the physical print as well as the file

Where the issuer permits this printing workflow, choose the right paper and print at 100 per cent. Turn off fit-to-page scaling and any borderless enlargement. Measure one finished photo with a ruler before cutting the whole sheet; a correct resolution tag cannot stop a printer's settings from changing its size.

Check the required paper quality, number of copies and anything that must be written or stamped on the back. The UK's printed-photo rules, for example, require professional-standard prints and prohibit a cut-down version of a larger picture. A sheet that looks tidy does not establish that its source or printing process meets a passport authority's instructions.

The work stays in your browser

Selecting a photo, placing the marks, measuring the crop and writing the JPEG are local operations. No face model or remote processing service is involved. Load the tool and try its example while disconnected if you want to check that the crop and export work without an upload.

The final submission is a separate step in the issuer's own application. Keep the original and check the exact file you plan to submit there. A geometry check here cannot approve the application or override its current instructions.
